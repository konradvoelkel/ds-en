## Decisions and doubts

**1. "the three producers of the wine" (block c4.5) contradicts the dataset documentation
and `descriptive-statistics.md`.** The German says the three classes are *"die drei
Hersteller von Wein"*, and I translated that faithfully. But the `wine.names` quote two
blocks above, in the same cell, says the wines are "derived from three different
cultivars" – grape varieties, not producers – and the already-translated
`descriptive-statistics.md` says, pointing at this very chapter, that "the colour carries
the grape variety in the wine dataset". So the English edition now says *producers* here
and *grape variety* there, about the same column. **This is a factual slip in the German
original, not a translation choice, and a human has to decide it.** If it is corrected, the
fix belongs upstream in the German first; the English block then becomes "the three grape
varieties" and the two chapters agree. I did not fix it unilaterally because that would put
content in the English edition that the German does not have.

**2. *Hauptachsentransformation (principal component analysis, PCA)* collapses to
*principal component analysis (PCA)* (block c23.1).** The German names the method with the
German term and then glosses it with the English one in brackets – the
*Störvariable/Confounder* pattern, where the bracket exists only for the German reader. In
English the bracket would repeat the name, so it goes, and "PCA" is kept because the rest
of the book uses the abbreviation. What is lost is the geometric flavour of
*Hauptachsentransformation* (the principal-axis transformation of a quadratic form), which
is the German's hint at *why* the method is coordinate-independent – the point of the
sentence. An alternative worth considering at the style pass: "with the principal axis
transformation, better known as principal component analysis (PCA)". The glossary has
*Hauptkomponentenanalyse → principal component analysis* and no row for
*Hauptachsentransformation*, which is why I did not invent one.

**3. *Prävalenzfehler* → *base rate fallacy* (block c59.0).** `TERMINOLOGY.md` binds
*Basisratenfehler* to **base rate fallacy** (from `covariance.md`), but this chapter uses a
different German word for the same thing, *Prävalenzfehler*, literally "prevalence error".
English has one established name, and `covariance.md` and `titanic-eda-numpy.ipynb` both
already use it for exactly this confusion of $P(A|B)$ with $P(B|A)$, so the two German words
collapse onto it. Recorded here rather than silently, because a reader comparing the
editions will see one English term where the German has two.

**4. The DWD's dataset citation stays German (block c24.2), with a `check-ignore` entry.**
The CC-licensed CatRaRE data must be cited as published, and what the DOI
`10.5676/DWD/CatRaRE_W3_Eta_v2021.01` resolves to is the German title *Starkregenereignisse
Version 2021.01 mit Überschreitung der DWD-Warnstufe 3 für Unwetter basierend auf
RADKLIM-RW Version 2017.002*. Translating it would make the attribution unverifiable, so it
is quoted verbatim (TRANSLATION.md §3, titles stay German) and `check_english.py` is
silenced for that block with a reason. The **link texts** around it are translated and, since
all three DWD pages are German-only, marked "(in German)" per TRANSLATION.md §3. The *URLs
themselves were not changed* – including `…doi_landingpage_CatRaRE_V2021.01-de.html`, which
very likely has an `-en.html` sibling, and `…/DE/leistungen/catrare/catrare.html`, which
very likely has an `/EN/ourservices/…` one. **A human with network access should check
those two and re-point them if the English pages exist**; I had no way to verify, and
guessing a URL is worse than marking the German one.

**5. The DWD's description blockquote (block c24.1) *was* translated.** It is the only
German blockquote in the chapter that carries information rather than serving as an example
of German usage: it tells the reader what the catalogue is. So "Kataloge radar-basierter
starker Niederschlagsereignisse als Grundlage für die Analyse des Starkregengeschehens und
seines Schadenpotenzials in Deutschland seit 2001" became "Catalogues of radar-based heavy
precipitation events, as a basis for analysing heavy rainfall activity and its damage
potential in Germany since 2001". The DWD publishes its own English wording for CatRaRE and
it may differ in detail; if the style pass can reach the English page, using the DWD's own
sentence would be better than mine. The citation in c24.2 and the English `wine.names`
quote in c4.3 were, by contrast, left exactly as they stood.

**6. `klasse` → `wine_class`, not `cls` or `klass`.** `class` is a keyword, so some
qualifier was unavoidable. `wine_class` says what it is, is the only name the section needs,
and sits beside the pre-existing `alcohol_by_class`. The commented-out line was renamed with
it (`klassenzahl` → `class_counts`) so that the comment still reads as the Python-list
version of the code beneath it, which is its entire purpose.

**7. *Anteil des Monats … in Prozent* → *Share of the month … in per cent* (cell 28).**
`densities` is `100 * counts / len(data)`, so the axis really is a percentage, and
TRANSLATION.md §6 asks for British "per cent". The variable is called `densities` although
it holds percentages – the German's own inconsistency, inside one code cell, left alone
because renaming it is not translation.

**8. *bloße Häufigkeit* → *bare frequency* (block c27.0).** "Sheer frequency" and "frequency
alone" were the alternatives. The sense is the univariate count before any covariate is
brought in, which is exactly what the next sentences then split by month. Flagged as the
one phrase in the chapter I am least sure reads as English.

**9. What a human must check, in order of risk.** (a) The producers-versus-cultivars slip
in c4.5, item 1 above – it is the only place where the English edition is now internally
inconsistent, and the inconsistency is inherited. (b) The two DWD URLs with likely English
siblings, item 4. (c) The stored outputs of cells 49 and 51 and the 38 stored PNGs are still
German; the re-execution pass must reach this notebook, and it needs network access to UCI
*and* to `opendata.dwd.de` – cell 25 downloads a 60-column CSV of some tens of MB at
execution time, which is by far the most fragile thing in the chapter. (d) `duessel` in
cell 30, kept deliberately, item in *Code changes*. (e) `Hauptachsentransformation`,
item 2, if the style pass wants the geometric hint back.
